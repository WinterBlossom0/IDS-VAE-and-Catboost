\begin{abstract}
Intrusion detectors often report near-perfect scores on random splits yet miss attack families absent from training. We propose a hybrid framework in which a robust variational autoencoder (VAE)---with a Student-t likelihood, missing-entry handling for undefined flow statistics, and a topological loss---feeds a CatBoost classifier and drives two streaming components. Using the VAE's reconstruction error and latent space, a correction relabels bursts of novel flows with the dominant recent attack class, while a detector flags unseen attack families. On CSE-CIC-IDS2018 with a time-ordered hold-out across three seeds, the framework reaches a mean F1 of \pmsd{\OursMean}{\OursSd} over the four larger classes, versus \pmsd{\RawMean}{\RawSd} for raw-feature CatBoost and \BestBaselineMean\ for the best of ten untuned re-run methods. This gain comes from the correction on the VAE signals, which also lifts raw-feature CatBoost to \RawCorrMean. With the detector, trained on attacks from other days, the framework reaches a Youden's $J$ of \JOursDaysEqual\,\% on left-out days, versus \JEqualBestDays\,\% for the best of five re-run methods trained on the same days, and the detector flags \DetectorAloneDetection\,\% of an unseen family's flows, versus \RawDetectorTwoDetection\,\% on raw features without monotone constraints. The full pipeline takes 0.05\,ms per flow on one CPU thread.

\keywords{Intrusion detection \and Variational autoencoder \and Student-t likelihood \and CatBoost \and Novelty \and Unseen attack families}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Intrusion detection systems (IDS) trained on network flows must handle class imbalance, heavy-tailed flow statistics, and attacks absent from training. Hybrid pipelines combining feature selection, autoencoders, and gradient-boosted trees report recalls near 99\,\%~\cite{kang2022} and accuracies near 98\,\%~\cite{chen2025vae} on public flow datasets, but these figures can be optimistic: random splits place flows from one attack session into both training and test data, ports let a model learn the attack machines, and accuracy masks small classes~\cite{arp2022,engelen2021,dhooge2022,flood2024}. Moreover, because a classifier can output only seen classes, its detection can collapse on a new attack family~\cite{ferreira2020,sakr2026}.

This paper retains the hybrid design but changes the autoencoder's role. In our framework (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}), CatBoost with SHAP importance selects the features (Stage~1), a robust variational autoencoder (VAE) encodes them (Stage~2), and a CatBoost classifier predicts the class from the VAE outputs (Stage~3). The VAE outputs also indicate how unusual a flow is, while its latent space groups similar flows; Stage~3 uses these signals in a streaming correction and an unseen-attack detector. We call Stages 1--3 without the detector \emph{the framework}, and write \emph{with the detector} when it is added. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A robust VAE design for CICFlowMeter flow features that treats undefined statistics as missing entries rather than zeros, models heavy tails with a Student-t likelihood, and adds a topological loss for latent neighbourhoods; its NLL and latent space feed Stage~3 (Sect.~\ref{sec:method}).
\item A streaming correction that relabels bursts of novel flows and a monotone CatBoost detector for unseen families; both run online without host addresses (Sect.~\ref{sec:method}).
\item An evaluation on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 on a hold-out, left-out days, leave-one-attack-out folds, and a test day against ten re-run methods, with ablations and CPU timing (Sect.~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Detection varies widely---and can collapse---when test attack classes are absent from training~\cite{ferreira2020,sakr2026,verkerken2023,soltani2021} (Table~\ref{tab:literature}, bottom). Prior hybrids pass autoencoder errors~\cite{kang2022} or VAE features~\cite{chen2025vae} to tree classifiers, while streaming detectors track per-host statistics~\cite{mirsky2018}, score bursts of edges between hosts~\cite{bhatia2020}, or classify flow windows~\cite{manocchio2024}. Instead, our streaming components count bursts in the VAE's latent space and thus require no host addresses.

\subsection{Stage 1: CatBoost with SHAP Feature Selection}

Stage~1 ranks the 64 candidate features by mean absolute SHAP value~\cite{lundberg2017} across 20 CatBoost runs, each on a random 70\,\% of the features, and retains the smallest top set holding 95\,\% of the importance: 44 features on our data (Fig.~\ref{fig:shap}, Table~\ref{tab:settings}).

\subsection{Stage 2: Robust Variational Autoencoder}

Each part of our VAE~\cite{kingma2014} addresses a property of flow statistics.

\paragraph{Undefined Statistics.}
CICFlowMeter writes 0 when it cannot compute a statistic~\cite{cicflowmeter}; for instance, an inter-arrival time requires two packets and an idle time requires an idle period. Such a zero is not a measurement. In the training rows, \UndefinedIdleMax\,\% of \emph{Idle Max} values and \UndefinedFlowIatStd\,\% of \emph{Flow IAT Std} values are undefined. We mark these entries as missing ($b_j=0$) using rules from the CICFlowMeter source code. Each feature $j$ has its own small encoder, an expert, mapping $x_j$ to a Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\mu_j,\sigma_j^2)$ over the latent vector $z\in\mathbb{R}^5$. The posterior is the product of the defined-feature experts and a standard normal prior expert~\cite{wu2018}: $\sigma^{-2}=1+\sum_j b_j\sigma_j^{-2}$ and $\mu=\sigma^{2}\sum_j b_j\sigma_j^{-2}\mu_j$. Thus, a missing feature neither affects the posterior nor adds a likelihood term~\cite{ma2019eddi,ivanov2019}.

\paragraph{Heavy Tails.}
In the training rows, flow durations span six orders of magnitude and forward packet counts span four, so a few extreme flows dominate a Gaussian likelihood. The decoder therefore assigns each feature a Student-t likelihood with learned location, scale, and degrees of freedom~\cite{takahashi2018}, with negative log-likelihood $\ell_j$. A soft lower bound on the log scale prevents the scale from collapsing~\cite{rybkin2021}.

\paragraph{Neighbourhoods.}
Because the Stage~3 correction counts flows in latent-space cells, similar flows must remain close after encoding, which the VAE objective does not ensure. We therefore add the 0-dimensional topological loss $\mathcal{L}_t$ of Moor et al.~\cite{moor2020}, comparing the minimum spanning trees of the inputs and posterior means within each batch.

\paragraph{Objective.}
To train the experts on incomplete rows, each batch also includes a second view $x_s$ per row that retains a random subset $s$ of the defined features~\cite{ma2019eddi,ivanov2019}. The VAE minimizes
\begin{multline}
\mathcal{L}=\sum_{v\in\{x,x_s\}}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{q(z\mid v)}\big[\textstyle\sum_j m^{v}_j\ell_j\big]+\beta\,\mathrm{KL}\big(q(z\mid v)\,\|\,p(z)\big)\Big)\\
+0.1\beta\,\mathrm{KL}\big(q(z\mid x_s)\,\|\,q(z\mid x)\big)+\gamma\mathcal{L}_t,
\end{multline}
where $m^v_j$ is 1 for features present in view $v$, $p(z)=\mathcal{N}(0,I)$, and the third term aligns the two views~\cite{sinha2021}. The weight is $\gamma=\topoweight$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:selection}).

\subsection{Stage 3: Classification, Correction and Detection}

\paragraph{Classifier.}
For each flow, the VAE produces seven outputs: the five posterior means $\mu$, the KL term, and the reconstruction NLL at $z=\mu$, summed over the \AlwaysDefinedCount\ features defined for every flow so that the sum has the same terms across flows. A multiclass CatBoost classifier~\cite{prokhorenkova2018} with balanced class weights predicts the class from these outputs and is tuned with Optuna~\cite{akiba2019}. Because the classifier knows only the training classes, in tests on unseen families we count any non-Benign class as an attack, as in the binary view of~\cite{ferreira2020}.

\paragraph{Novel-Burst Correction.}
A learned attack can recur in a form the classifier misses: Slowloris teardown flows appear Benign to the classifier, yet look unusual to the VAE and arrive in bursts. The correction identifies such flows in three steps. First, a flow is \emph{novel} if its reconstruction NLL exceeds the $q_n$ quantile of Benign NLLs on a calibration day. Second, a flow belongs to a \emph{burst} when many flows have recently arrived in its latent cell: after dividing each latent axis by its training SD, the cell is the grid cube of side $w$ containing the flow's posterior mean. We measure this with the MIDAS-R score~\cite{bhatia2020} thresholded at its $q_b$ quantile on Benign training flows, using the latent cell in place of the host pair since the flow records carry no addresses. Third, a novel flow in a burst adopts the attack class predicted most often by the classifier among earlier flows from the last $W$ seconds of the day, provided that class count passes a support threshold; otherwise, its prediction remains unchanged. Because the calibration day contains no training attack classes, any attack prediction on its Benign flows is a false alarm, and the $q_s$ quantile of this count over its Benign flows sets the threshold. All quantities use only earlier flows, so the correction runs online. In the hold-out evaluation, same-day training flows remain in the stream and count as Benign predictions so that no training label adds attack support.

\paragraph{Unseen-Attack Detector.}
A flow from a new attack family that resembles no learned class remains Benign after the correction, so a second CatBoost model, the detector, checks every Benign flow. Its first input is the \emph{relative NLL}: the reconstruction NLL minus the running median NLL of earlier same-day non-burst flows, since days differ in their normal NLL level. The other four inputs are the KL term, the latent cell's MIDAS-R score, the same score over novel flows only (relative NLL above its $q_n$ quantile on calibration-day Benign flows), and the novel-flow share within the cell over the last $W$ seconds. Each input is replaced by its percentile rank among Benign reference flows so that all days share a common scale. Monotone constraints~\cite{incer2018} allow larger inputs only to raise the attack score, ensuring a more unusual flow never gets a lower score and making the detector less able to learn a narrow value range of one training family.

Its positives are labelled attacks from families absent from the VAE and classifier training labels: the training rows of the left-out class in each leave-one-attack-out fold, encoded by that fold's models, plus the attacks of day 02-28 and further capture days (Sect.~\ref{sec:protocol}). The negatives are half of the Benign flows from these days and the calibration day, with the cut-off set for 1\,\% false alarms on the other half. A left-out day excludes that day and its families on all days from detector training; a fold excludes its class. Any flagged flow becomes an \emph{unknown attack}, a wrong class in the seen-class metrics.

\subsection{Hyperparameter Selection}\label{sec:selection}

No model weight or threshold is fitted on the hold-out or test day, and the VAE stops on the 02-28 ELBO. The topological weight $\gamma$ and the five correction settings are chosen with seed~3 on an inner split holding out the latest 20\,\% of training rows per day and class. The weight $\gamma$ maximizes mean per-class F1 with the correction at its start values. The correction settings are then tuned one by one to maximize mean attack-versus-Benign F1 across three leave-one-attack-out folds of the inner split. For the detector, the NLL is taken relative to either the calibration-day median NLL or the day's running median, and the inputs are chosen from five subsets; each configuration is validated by leaving out one further capture day or one fold at a time. The chosen configuration---the running median with all five inputs---achieves the highest mean detection rate among those with at most 2\,\% mean false alarms. Table~\ref{tab:settings} lists the grids and selected values.

\section{Experiments and Results}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Dataset and Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

We used the official CSE-CIC-IDS2018 CSV files~\cite{cic2018page,sharafaldin2018}, corrected their 12-hour CICFlowMeter timestamps~\cite{cicflowmeter}, and removed rows with non-finite values. To prevent shortcuts, we dropped the timestamp~\cite{engelen2021,flood2024}, the destination port~\cite{dhooge2022}, and three TCP header fields that identify the attacker machines on the training days (\emph{Init Fwd Win Byts}, \emph{Init Bwd Win Byts}, \emph{Fwd Seg Size Min}; similar shortcuts are noted in~\cite{flood2024,engelen2021}), along with \ConstantCount\ constant columns, leaving 64 candidate features. We also removed duplicate rows, rows from other days matching training rows, and label conflicts (identical features with different labels). These conflicts include \FtpConflictRows\ FTP brute-force rows, consistent with the failed FTP attack in~\cite{liu2022error}.

Table~\ref{tab:data} summarizes the splits. Because the classes of test day 02-21 are absent from training, this day tests unseen families. Its HOIC window contains a burst of \BurstRows\ Benign flows of one template; since Liu et al.~\cite{liu2022error} report no HOIC label issue beyond a time offset, we kept these flows as Benign. Outside this burst, the day has only \TestBenignOutside\ Benign flows. The detector also trains on further capture days where at least six hours each hold at least 1\,\% of the day's Benign flows, a rule that excludes the truncated file of day 02-16. On day 02-20, we removed \MislabelledUdpRows\ UDP flows labelled LOIC-HTTP within the LOIC-UDP window, since LOIC-UDP is a test-day class.

\paragraph{Metrics and Runs.}
For seen classes, the main metric is mean per-class F1 over classes with at least 30 hold-out rows, while FTP (8 rows) is reported separately. For unseen families, we report the detection rate (DR), Benign false-positive rate (FPR), and Youden's $J=\mathrm{DR}-\mathrm{FPR}$. Each seed (3, 4, 5) retrains every model after Stage~1 and recomputes every threshold, while Stage~1 runs once on the main split and once per fold. We report the mean and standard deviation (SD) over seeds and a paired bootstrap of the difference to raw CatBoost~\cite{koehn2004}. The VAEs and PyTorch re-run methods were trained on an RTX 5080 Laptop GPU and the other models on an Intel Core Ultra 9 275HX CPU; the code repository gives library versions, thread counts, and the command for every table: \url{https://github.com/WinterBlossom0/IDS-VAE-and-Catboost}.

\subsection{Seen Classes: The Correction on VAE Signals Gives the Gain}

We first test whether Stage~2 and the correction improve on CatBoost with the same 44 features (raw CatBoost) on the four classes with at least 30 hold-out rows (Table~\ref{tab:main}). The framework reaches a mean per-class F1 of \pmsd{\OursMean}{\OursSd}, versus \pmsd{\RawMean}{\RawSd} for raw CatBoost and \BestBaselineMean\ for the best re-run method. It beats raw CatBoost in every seed by \GainMin\ to \GainMax\ (paired bootstrap, 95\,\% intervals for seeds 3, 4, and 5: \BootstrapIntervals). With the detector, F1 drops only to \pmsd{\DetectorMean}{\DetectorSd}, as the detector flags about 1\,\% of Benign hold-out flows as unknown attacks. Table~\ref{tab:perclass} shows that the gain comes from Slowloris, while raw CatBoost is better on FTP (8 hold-out rows) and thus over all five classes (\AllClassRaw\ versus \AllClassOurs).

The ablations in Table~\ref{tab:main} pinpoint the gain. Without the correction, the VAE classifier trails raw CatBoost (\NoCorrMean\ versus \RawMean). The correction raises its Slowloris recall from \SlowRecallBase\ (not tabulated) to \SlowRecallCorr, and lifts raw CatBoost to a mean F1 of \RawCorrMean\ too, tying the framework within the seed spread. Still, raw CatBoost with the correction must run the VAE for the NLL and latent cells, relabels more Benign flows, and is slower than the framework. The gain thus comes from the correction, which requires the VAE signals, rather than from the VAE as a feature compressor. Accordingly, a Gaussian likelihood, no topological loss, or the latent mean alone each changes the F1 by at most \VaePartGap\ with the correction present, within the seed spread, whereas without the correction the topological loss lowers the F1. For unseen families, a detector on the VAE without the topological loss reaches $J$ \JArmBase\,\% on the left-out days, versus \JArmTopology\,\% with it, when both train on the further days only, so the loss does not alter detection either. LightGBM and XGBoost in Stage~3 match the F1 with lower balanced accuracy. Giving Stage~3 both the 44 raw features and the seven VAE outputs fails to close the gap before the correction (\ConcatNoCorrMean\ versus \RawMean) and matches the framework after it (\ConcatMean), while removing the FTP false alarms (FTP F1 \ConcatFtpScore\ versus \OursFtpScore).

For LOIC-HTTP, it flags \LoicRequestDetection\,\% of request flows but only \LoicNoReplyDetection\,\% of unanswered attempts, while Infiltration stays mostly undetected. The VAE-based inputs are key: a CatBoost detector on the 44 raw features instead of its five inputs, trained on the same rows with the same cut-off but without monotone constraints, flags on average only \RawDetectorDetection\,\% (depth 6) and \RawDetectorTwoDetection\,\% (depth 2) of each left-out family's flows, versus \DetectorAloneDetection\,\% for ours (mean of Table~\ref{tab:detectordays}a), showing that the labelled further days and tree depth alone do not explain the detection.

Table~\ref{tab:unseen} compares all methods by Youden's $J$. Trained on days 02-14 and 02-15 only, our classifier scores like most re-run methods ($J$ \JClassifierDays\,\% on left-out days); with the detector, also trained on the further days, the framework reaches \JOursDays\,\% and \JOursFolds\,\% in the folds. Since the detector configuration was selected on these days and folds (Sect.~\ref{sec:selection}), only the test day is unused by that choice. The test day shows the limit of the burst signal: 99.4\,\% of its Benign flows belong to one burst inside the HOIC window. The framework detects \TestUdpMean\,\% of LOIC-UDP flows, but also flags \TestBurstFprMean\,\% of this Benign burst, which receives higher detector scores than the HOIC flows. In the rows marked $^+$ in Table~\ref{tab:unseen}, the shallow re-run methods also train on the further days except the evaluated one, with each attack flow labelled by family, and flag flows by the detector's cut-off rule; Isolation Forest~\cite{liu2008}, an open-set detector, trains on their Benign flows only. Given the same days, the best reach $J$ \JEqualBestDays\,\% on left-out days (\JEqualBestDaysName) and \JEqualBestFolds\,\% in the folds (\JEqualBestFoldsName), versus \JOursDaysEqual\,\% and \JOursFoldsEqual\,\%.

\section{Limits}\label{sec:discussion}
Unusual Benign bursts mimic attacks, non-burst attacks give no signal, and the correction assumes one attack at a time.
Our evidence comes from one dataset with known errors and label faults~\cite{engelen2021,liu2022error} and three seeds. CNN-1D, FlowTransformer, TabM, FT-Transformer, and TabNet were not given the further days, nor was Kitsune~\cite{mirsky2018} run; re-run methods use defaults without the correction, and no ablation removes missing-entry handling.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We proposed a hybrid VAE-CatBoost framework in which a robust VAE serves both a classifier and two streaming components. On CSE-CIC-IDS2018, the correction lifts the mean per-class F1 of the four larger classes above raw CatBoost and ten untuned re-run methods, and the framework with the detector reaches a Youden's $J$ of \JOursDaysEqual\,\% on left-out days, versus \JEqualBestDays\,\% for the best untuned re-run method given the same days. A large Benign burst on the test day defeats the detector; separating such bursts from attacks remains future work.
