\begin{abstract}
Network intrusion models often perform well on random data splits yet still miss attack families that were excluded from training. We introduce a hybrid architecture in which a robust variational autoencoder (VAE), with a Student-t likelihood, explicit masking of undefined flow statistics, and a topological loss, feeds a CatBoost classifier and two online modules. Using the VAE's negative log-likelihood and latent coordinates, a streaming correction relabels bursts of novel flows with the most frequent recent attack prediction, while a secondary detector flags unseen attack families. On CSE-CIC-IDS2018 with a chronological hold-out across three seeds, our approach achieves a mean per-class F1 of \pmsd{\OursMean}{\OursSd} over the four larger classes, compared with \pmsd{\RawMean}{\RawSd} for raw-feature CatBoost and \BestBaselineMean\ for the strongest of ten untuned re-run methods. The same VAE-driven correction also raises raw-feature CatBoost to \RawCorrMean, confirming that the correction acting on VAE signals drives the gain. When trained on attacks from other days, the framework with the detector reaches a Youden's $J$ of \JOursDaysEqual\,\% on left-out days, versus \JEqualBestDays\,\% for the best of five re-run methods trained on the same days. The detector alone flags \DetectorAloneDetection\,\% of an unseen family's flows, versus \RawDetectorTwoDetection\,\% on raw features without monotone constraints. Single-thread CPU inference of the full pipeline takes 0.05\,ms per flow.

\keywords{Intrusion detection \and Variational autoencoder \and Student-t likelihood \and CatBoost \and Novelty \and Unseen attack families}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Flow-based intrusion detection systems (IDS) must cope with skewed classes, extreme outliers in traffic statistics, and attack families that are absent from training. Hybrids of feature selection, autoencoders, and gradient-boosted trees report recalls near 99\,\%~\cite{kang2022} and accuracies near 98\,\%~\cite{chen2025vae} on public flow datasets, but such figures can be optimistic. Random partitioning places flows from one attack session in both training and test sets; port numbers let a model memorize attacker hosts; and accuracy hides weak rare classes~\cite{arp2022,engelen2021,dhooge2022,flood2024}. A closed-set classifier also outputs only known labels, so its detection can collapse on a new attack family~\cite{ferreira2020,sakr2026}.

We keep the multi-stage hybrid design but redefine what the autoencoder does. In our framework (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}), Stage~1 selects features using CatBoost SHAP values. Stage~2 encodes them through a robust variational autoencoder (VAE), and Stage~3 classifies each flow with CatBoost from the VAE outputs. The VAE's reconstruction NLL measures how unusual a flow is, while its latent space groups similar traffic; Stage~3 feeds both signals into an online burst correction and an unseen-attack detector. We call Stages 1--3 without the detector \emph{the framework}, and say \emph{with the detector} when it is added. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A robust VAE design for CICFlowMeter flow features that treats undefined statistics as missing entries rather than zeros, models heavy tails with a Student-t likelihood, and adds a topological loss for latent neighbourhoods; its NLL and latent space feed Stage~3 (Sect.~\ref{sec:method}).
\item A streaming correction that relabels bursts of novel flows and a monotone CatBoost detector for unseen families; both run online without host addresses (Sect.~\ref{sec:method}).
\item An evaluation on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 on a hold-out, left-out days, leave-one-attack-out folds, and a test day against ten re-run methods, with ablations and CPU timing (Sect.~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Detection varies substantially across prior studies and can fall sharply when test attack classes are absent from training~\cite{ferreira2020,sakr2026,verkerken2023,soltani2021} (Table~\ref{tab:literature}, bottom). Earlier hybrids pass autoencoder errors~\cite{kang2022} or latent VAE representations~\cite{chen2025vae} to tree classifiers. Streaming detectors instead maintain per-host state~\cite{mirsky2018}, score edge bursts between host pairs~\cite{bhatia2020}, or classify multi-flow windows with transformers~\cite{manocchio2024}. Our streaming components track bursts in the VAE latent space and need no IP addresses.

\subsection{Stage 1: CatBoost with SHAP Feature Selection}

Stage~1 ranks the 64 candidate features by mean absolute SHAP value~\cite{lundberg2017} across 20 CatBoost runs, each on a random 70\,\% of the features, and retains the smallest top set holding 95\,\% of the importance: 44 features on our data (Fig.~\ref{fig:shap}, Table~\ref{tab:settings}).

\subsection{Stage 2: Robust Variational Autoencoder}

Each part of our VAE~\cite{kingma2014} addresses a property of flow statistics.

\paragraph{Undefined Statistics.}
When CICFlowMeter cannot compute a statistic---for example, an inter-arrival time from a single packet or an idle time when no idle period exists---it writes a default value of 0~\cite{cicflowmeter}. These zeros are missing entries, not measurements. In the training rows, \UndefinedIdleMax\,\% of \emph{Idle Max} values and \UndefinedFlowIatStd\,\% of \emph{Flow IAT Std} values are undefined. Following the CICFlowMeter source code, we mark such entries as missing ($b_j=0$). Each feature $j$ has its own small encoder, an expert, mapping $x_j$ to a Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\mu_j,\sigma_j^2)$ over the latent vector $z\in\mathbb{R}^5$. The posterior is the product of the defined-feature experts and a standard normal prior expert~\cite{wu2018}: $\sigma^{-2}=1+\sum_j b_j\sigma_j^{-2}$ and $\mu=\sigma^{2}\sum_j b_j\sigma_j^{-2}\mu_j$. Thus, a missing feature neither affects the posterior nor adds a likelihood term~\cite{ma2019eddi,ivanov2019}.

\paragraph{Heavy Tails.}
Because training flow durations span six orders of magnitude and forward packet counts span four, a few extreme outliers could otherwise dominate a standard Gaussian likelihood. The decoder therefore gives each feature a Student-t likelihood with learned location, scale, and degrees of freedom~\cite{takahashi2018}, and negative log-likelihood $\ell_j$. A soft lower bound on the log scale keeps the scale from collapsing~\cite{rybkin2021}.

\paragraph{Neighbourhoods.}
Stage~3's burst correction counts flows within latent grid cells. That requires similar traffic to remain close after encoding---a property the standard VAE objective does not enforce. We therefore add the 0-dimensional topological loss $\mathcal{L}_t$ of Moor et al.~\cite{moor2020}, comparing minimum spanning trees of the inputs and posterior means within each 256-flow sub-batch.

\paragraph{Objective.}
To train the experts on incomplete rows, each batch also includes a second view $x_s$ per row that retains a random subset $s$ of the defined features~\cite{ma2019eddi,ivanov2019}. The VAE minimizes
\begin{multline}
\mathcal{L}=\sum_{v\in\{x,x_s\}}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{q(z\mid v)}\big[\textstyle\sum_j m^{v}_j\ell_j\big]+\beta\,\mathrm{KL}\big(q(z\mid v)\,\|\,p(z)\big)\Big)\\
+0.1\beta\,\mathrm{KL}\big(q(z\mid x_s)\,\|\,q(z\mid x)\big)+\gamma\mathcal{L}_t,
\end{multline}
where $m^v_j$ is 1 for features present in view $v$, $p(z)=\mathcal{N}(0,I)$, and the third term aligns the two views~\cite{sinha2021}. The weight is $\gamma=\topoweight$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:selection}).

\subsection{Stage 3: Classification, Correction and Detection}

\paragraph{Classifier.}
For each flow, the VAE produces seven outputs: the five posterior means $\mu$, the KL term, and the reconstruction NLL at $z=\mu$, summed over the \AlwaysDefinedCount\ features defined for every flow so that the sum has the same terms across flows. A multiclass CatBoost classifier~\cite{prokhorenkova2018} with balanced class weights predicts the class from these outputs and is tuned with Optuna~\cite{akiba2019}. Since the classifier outputs only training labels, evaluations on unseen attack families treat any non-Benign prediction as a detected attack, following the binary convention of~\cite{ferreira2020}.

\paragraph{Novel-Burst Correction.}
A learned attack can recur in a form the classifier misses: Slowloris teardown flows appear Benign to the classifier, yet look unusual to the VAE and arrive in bursts. The correction works in three steps. First, we mark a flow as \emph{novel} when its reconstruction NLL is above the $q_n$ quantile of Benign NLLs on a calibration day. Second, a flow belongs to a \emph{burst} when many flows have recently arrived in its latent cell. After dividing each latent axis by its training SD, the cell is the grid cube of side $w$ that contains the flow's posterior mean. Because the flow records lack IP addresses, MIDAS-R~\cite{bhatia2020} evaluates the latent cell in place of a host pair and flags a burst whenever its score exceeds the $q_b$ quantile of Benign training flows. Third, a novel flow inside a burst adopts the attack class most frequently predicted by the classifier among earlier flows over the preceding $W$ seconds of the same day, provided that this class count clears a support threshold; otherwise, its prediction stays unchanged. Since the calibration day contains no training attack classes, any attack prediction on its Benign flows is a false alarm, and the $q_s$ quantile of these false-alarm counts sets the threshold. Every step uses only earlier flows, so the correction runs online. During the hold-out evaluation, same-day training flows remain in the stream and count as Benign predictions, so no training label adds attack support.

\paragraph{Unseen-Attack Detector.}
If a flow from an unseen attack family triggers no known-class prediction, the burst correction leaves it as Benign; a second CatBoost model, the detector, therefore examines every remaining Benign flow. Because the normal NLL level varies across capture days, its first input is the \emph{relative NLL}: the reconstruction NLL minus the running median NLL of earlier same-day flows whose MIDAS-R score is below its 0.9 Benign training quantile. The four additional inputs are the KL term, the latent cell's MIDAS-R score, a novel-only MIDAS-R score over flows whose relative NLL exceeds its $q_n$ quantile on calibration-day Benign flows, and the fraction of novel flows in the cell over the last $W$ seconds. Converting each input to its percentile rank among Benign reference flows puts all days on a shared scale. Monotone constraints~\cite{incer2018} ensure that a larger input never lowers the attack score and make it harder for the tree model to learn a narrow value range of one training family.

Its positives are labelled attacks from families absent from the VAE and classifier training labels: the training rows of the left-out class in each leave-one-attack-out fold, encoded by that fold's models, plus the attacks of day 02-28 and further capture days (Sect.~\ref{sec:protocol}). The negatives are half of the Benign flows from these days and the calibration day, with the cut-off set for 1\,\% false alarms on the other half. A left-out day excludes that day and its families on all days from detector training; a fold excludes its class. Any flagged flow becomes an \emph{unknown attack}, a wrong class in the seen-class metrics.

\subsection{Hyperparameter Selection}\label{sec:selection}

No model weight or threshold is fitted on the hold-out or test day, and the VAE stops on the 02-28 ELBO. The topological weight $\gamma$ and the five correction settings are chosen with seed~3 on an inner split holding out the latest 20\,\% of training rows per day and class. The weight $\gamma$ maximizes mean per-class F1 with the correction at its start values. The correction settings are then tuned one by one to maximize mean attack-versus-Benign F1 across three leave-one-attack-out folds of the inner split. For the detector, the NLL is taken relative to either the calibration-day median NLL or the day's running median, and the inputs are chosen from five subsets; each configuration is validated by leaving out one further capture day or one fold at a time. The selected setup---the running median with all five inputs---has the highest mean detection rate among configurations with at most 2\,\% mean false alarms. Table~\ref{tab:settings} gives the grids and selected values.

\section{Experiments and Results}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Dataset and Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

We used the official CSE-CIC-IDS2018 CSV files~\cite{cic2018page,sharafaldin2018}, corrected their 12-hour CICFlowMeter timestamps~\cite{cicflowmeter}, and removed rows with non-finite values. We also removed features that could enable shortcut learning: the timestamp~\cite{engelen2021,flood2024}, the destination port~\cite{dhooge2022}, \ConstantCount\ constant columns, and three TCP header fields that identify attacker hosts on the training days (\emph{Init Fwd Win Byts}, \emph{Init Bwd Win Byts}, \emph{Fwd Seg Size Min}; related shortcuts are discussed in~\cite{flood2024,engelen2021}). This left 64 candidate features. We filtered exact duplicates, cross-day matches of training rows, and label conflicts, i.e., identical feature vectors with different labels. The conflicts include \FtpConflictRows\ FTP brute-force rows, consistent with the failed FTP attack reported in~\cite{liu2022error}.

Table~\ref{tab:data} lists the resulting splits. Test day 02-21 contains only attack families excluded from training and is our unseen-family test set. Its HOIC window includes a single-template burst of \BurstRows\ Benign flows. Liu et al.~\cite{liu2022error} found no HOIC labelling issue beyond a time offset, so we kept these flows as Benign; only \TestBenignOutside\ Benign flows on that day fall outside this burst. The detector also trains on further capture days that contain at least six distinct hours, each with at least 1\,\% of the day's Benign flows; this rule excludes the truncated file for day 02-16. On day 02-20, we removed \MislabelledUdpRows\ UDP flows labelled LOIC-HTTP within the LOIC-UDP window, since LOIC-UDP is a test-day class.

\paragraph{Metrics and Runs.}
For seen classes, we report mean per-class F1 over classes with at least 30 hold-out rows and report the 8-row FTP class separately. For unseen families, we measure detection rate (DR), Benign false-positive rate (FPR), and Youden's $J=\mathrm{DR}-\mathrm{FPR}$. Stage~1 feature selection is run once on the main split and once per fold; all later models and thresholds are retrained for seeds 3, 4, and 5. We report the mean and standard deviation (SD) across seeds, together with a paired bootstrap comparison against raw CatBoost~\cite{koehn2004}. The VAEs and PyTorch re-run methods were trained on an RTX 5080 Laptop GPU and the other models on an Intel Core Ultra 9 275HX CPU; the code repository gives library versions, thread counts, and the command for every table: \url{https://github.com/WinterBlossom0/IDS-VAE-and-Catboost}.

\subsection{Seen Classes: The Correction on VAE Signals Gives the Gain}

We first compare the framework with a CatBoost model trained directly on the same 44 Stage~1 features (raw CatBoost) on the four seen classes with at least 30 hold-out rows (Table~\ref{tab:main}). The framework reaches a mean per-class F1 of \pmsd{\OursMean}{\OursSd}, higher than raw CatBoost (\pmsd{\RawMean}{\RawSd}) and the best re-run method (\BestBaselineMean). For seeds 3, 4, and 5, its margin over raw CatBoost is \GainMin--\GainMax, with 95\,\% paired bootstrap intervals \BootstrapIntervals. Turning on the detector lowers F1 slightly to \pmsd{\DetectorMean}{\DetectorSd} because it flags about 1\,\% of Benign hold-out flows as unknown attacks. Table~\ref{tab:perclass} shows that the gain comes from Slowloris; raw CatBoost does better on the 8-row FTP class and therefore over all five classes (\AllClassRaw\ versus \AllClassOurs). The ablations in Table~\ref{tab:main} locate the source of this gain. Without the correction, the VAE-fed classifier is below raw CatBoost (\NoCorrMean\ versus \RawMean). The correction raises its Slowloris recall from \SlowRecallBase\ (not tabulated) to \SlowRecallCorr\ and also lifts raw CatBoost to \RawCorrMean, which matches the framework within the seed spread. Even so, raw CatBoost with the correction still must run the VAE to compute NLLs and latent cells, relabels more Benign flows, and is slower than the framework. The gain therefore comes from the correction using VAE signals, not from the VAE serving as a feature compressor. Consistent with this, a Gaussian likelihood, removal of the topological loss, or use of latent means alone changes F1 by at most \VaePartGap\ with the correction, within the seed spread; without the correction, the topological loss lowers F1. On unseen families, a detector on a VAE without the topological loss reaches a left-out-day $J$ of \JArmBase\,\%, versus \JArmTopology\,\% with it, when both train only on the further days; the loss therefore does not change detection either.

Using LightGBM or XGBoost in Stage~3 gives a similar F1 but lower balanced accuracy. Concatenating the 44 raw features with the seven VAE outputs still trails raw CatBoost before correction (\ConcatNoCorrMean\ versus \RawMean), ties the framework after correction (\ConcatMean), and removes the FTP false alarms (FTP F1 \ConcatFtpScore\ versus \OursFtpScore).

\section{Limits}\label{sec:discussion}
Unusual Benign bursts can trigger false alarms, attacks without bursts give no signal, and the correction assumes one dominant attack class per time window.
The evidence comes from three seeds on one dataset with known errors and label faults~\cite{engelen2021,liu2022error}. The deep re-run methods (CNN-1D, FlowTransformer, TabM, FT-Transformer, TabNet) were not given the further days, Kitsune~\cite{mirsky2018} was not run, re-run methods use defaults without the correction, and missing-entry handling was not ablated.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We proposed a hybrid VAE-CatBoost framework in which a robust VAE serves both a classifier and two streaming components. On CSE-CIC-IDS2018, the correction lifts the mean per-class F1 of the four larger classes above raw CatBoost and ten untuned re-run methods, and the framework with the detector reaches a Youden's $J$ of \JOursDaysEqual\,\% on left-out days, versus \JEqualBestDays\,\% for the best untuned re-run method given the same days. A large Benign burst on the test day defeats the detector; separating such bursts from attacks remains future work.
